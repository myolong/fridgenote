A good babysitter sheet answers questions before they come up. It also gives the sitter something to point to at bedtime when a 6-year-old insists that Mom always allows three books.

Start with emergencies

Fill this part in first, even if you skip everything else.

  • "Call 911 first, then call us." Saying it plainly removes any doubt.
  • Your home address. Write the full address the way a dispatcher would need it.
  • Poison Control. In the US it's 1-800-222-1222, free and open 24 hours.
  • Your pediatrician's name and number.
  • A backup adult who lives close. Someone who can be at your door in minutes.
  • Where the first aid kit is.

Then, each child

FoodPasta in the fridge, 1 minute in the microwave
Bedtime routineBath, pajamas, 2 books, lights out 8:00
ComfortBunny named Pip. Leave the nightlight on.
Good to knowWill ask for a 3rd book. One more is fine.

The "good to know" line is underrated. Telling a sitter what's normal for your child, like crying for a few minutes after lights out, saves them from calling you in the middle of dinner.

Tonight's plan

Four lines with times are enough: dinner, bath, and bedtime for each child. Our sheet prints these with checkboxes.

House rules

  • Screens: how long, and which apps or profiles
  • Snacks and sweets
  • Going outside, especially after dark
  • Opening the door to visitors
  • Anything the sitter is welcome to, like snacks in the fridge

Around the house

  • Wi-Fi, or a Wi-Fi QR card
  • Which TV profile or remote to use
  • Flashlight, pajamas, spare diapers
  • Pets, and whether any doors must stay closed

What not to put on the fridge

Door codes, alarm codes, and garage codes. A sheet on the fridge can be seen by anyone who comes in. Tell your sitter in person or send a text.
