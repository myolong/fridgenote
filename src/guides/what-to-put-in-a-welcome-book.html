A welcome book cuts down on messages, and guests notice the effort. The trick is to keep it short. People skim on arrival, so lead with what they need in the first ten minutes.

Page 1: a warm welcome and the Wi-Fi

  • A short welcome message in your own words
  • Your name, signed like a note
  • The Wi-Fi network and password, ideally with a QR code guests can scan

Getting settled

  • Check-in time
  • Parking: where, how many cars, any street rules
  • Trash and recycling: where the bins are and pickup days

How things work

Write one short entry for each thing guests ask about. Common ones:

ItemWhat guests want to know
ThermostatWhere it is and what range is fine
TVWhich remote, and how to sign in to their own streaming accounts
Coffee makerWhere the filters and coffee are
Washer and dryerWhere detergent is and any quirks
Fireplace, hot tub, grillHow to turn on and off, and any safety notes

A good test: if three guests have messaged you the same question, it goes in the book.

House rules

Keep them short and friendly. Quiet hours, smoking, pets, the number of guests, and anything specific to your place, like shoes off at the door. See house rules examples for wording.

Local favorites

This is where a welcome book feels personal. List four to eight places you actually like, with one line about why: "Get the cherry scone. Opens at 7." Guests can find a list of restaurants on their phone. They can't find your opinion.

Before you leave

Checkout time and four or five simple tasks. More on this in check-out instructions examples.

If you need anything

  • The property's full address, for emergency calls
  • Your contact, and whether to text or call
  • A local backup contact if you live far away
  • The nearest hospital or urgent care
  • Where the first aid kit, fire extinguisher, and water shutoff are

What to leave out

  • Door and lockbox codes (send them privately)
  • Long paragraphs about the history of the house
  • Rules that read like a contract. Keep the tone friendly.

Short-term rental rules, taxes, and safety requirements vary by city and state. Check your local rules separately. A welcome book doesn't replace them.
