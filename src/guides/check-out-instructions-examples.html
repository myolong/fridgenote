Most guests want to leave the place in good shape. What they don't know is what "good shape" means to you. Does the dishwasher need to run? Do towels go in the tub or stay on the rack? A short list answers those questions before anyone has to ask.

A 6-step example

This is the kind of list that fits on the back page of a welcome book or a small card by the door:

Check-out is 11 AM. Before you go:

  1. Load and start the dishwasher (pods are under the sink).
  2. Put used towels in the bathtub.
  3. Take kitchen trash to the gray bin by the garage.
  4. Set the thermostat to 65°F.
  5. Close and lock all windows and the back door.
  6. Leave the key on the kitchen counter and lock the front door behind you.

That's it. Thanks for staying with us, and safe travels!

Six steps, all doable in about ten minutes. Each one names a place ("under the sink", "by the garage") so nobody has to search.

What to leave out

  • Full cleaning. Vacuuming, mopping, or cleaning the bathroom is usually the cleaner's job. Asking guests to do it can feel unfair if there's also a cleaning fee.
  • Long paragraphs. A numbered list is easier to follow while carrying bags.
  • Warnings in capital letters. "Please" and a short reason get better results than "DO NOT".

Where guests will see it

Put the list on the last page of your welcome book, under its own heading. Then print the same list as a small card and leave it by the front door or on the kitchen counter. Guests look for it on the morning they leave, not when they arrive.
