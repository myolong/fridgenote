How to use it

  1. List what to do each visit. Four or five short tasks is plenty. Each one gets a checkbox on the printout.
  2. Add plants, trash days, and the basics of the house. The water shutoff and breaker panel are worth a line even if nothing goes wrong.
  3. Print it and walk through the house together. Show your sitter where the shutoff and breakers actually are.

What to cover

SectionWhy it matters
Each visitA short checklist keeps visits quick and consistent.
Plants"Water the plants" leads to drowned plants. Amounts and days don't.
Trash, mail & deliveriesA pile of mail or a bin left out for a week tells passersby nobody's home.
Heat, water & powerA burst pipe is the most expensive thing that can happen while you're gone. Knowing where the shutoff is helps.
If something goes wrongYour address, a neighbor with a key, and the repair person you trust.

Tips

  • Write recycling dates, not just the day. "Every other Monday" is easy to get wrong. "Dec 22 and Jan 5" isn't.
  • Mention the time difference if you're traveling, so your sitter knows when it's OK to call.
  • Ask for a quick text after each visit. It's reassuring, and it reminds them the visit counts.
