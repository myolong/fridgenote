Vacation rental welcome book

Build a short, printable welcome book for your rental or guest room. It starts with a cover and a Wi-Fi QR code guests can scan.

What you type stays in this browser. Nothing is uploaded.

How to use it

  1. Write a short welcome and add your Wi-Fi. The cover gets a QR code guests can scan to join.
  2. Fill in the questions guests ask most. Heat, TV, coffee, trash, and checkout. Skip anything you don't need.
  3. Print double-sided and put it somewhere obvious. A binder or a clear sleeve on the kitchen counter works well.

Tips from hosts

  • Answer the questions you get by message. If three guests asked how the TV works, it belongs in the book.
  • Keep checkout short. Four or five simple tasks feel fair. A long list can sour the end of a nice stay.
  • Write local tips in your own words. "Get the cherry scone" is more useful than a list of restaurant names.
  • Keep codes out of print. Send door and lockbox codes by message, and change them between stays.
  • Check your local rules separately. Short-term rental rules, taxes, and safety requirements vary by city. This tool doesn't cover them.

Questions

What should a vacation rental welcome book include?
The basics guests ask about most: Wi-Fi, check-in and parking, how the heat and TV work, house rules, trash, checkout steps, and who to contact. A few local favorites make it feel personal.
Should I put the door code in the welcome book?
We'd recommend against it. Codes often change between stays, and a printed book can be read by anyone in the house. Send codes privately before arrival.
How long should it be?
Most guests skim. Two to four pages is plenty. The preview shows the page count as you type.
Can I use this for a guest room or a house swap?
Yes. Change the title and skip anything that doesn't apply, like check-in times.
Is this connected to any rental platform?
No. FridgeNote isn't affiliated with any booking site. It just makes a printable book from what you type, inside your browser.
