Babysitter information sheet

Everything your sitter needs for the evening on one page: the kids, the plan, the rules, and who to call.

What you type stays in this browser. Nothing is uploaded.

How to use it

  1. Start with the emergency box. Your numbers, your address, and a backup adult nearby.
  2. Add a box for each child. Allergies and bedtime routine matter most. Skip anything that doesn't apply.
  3. Print it and walk through it with your sitter. Five minutes at the door clears up most questions.

What a good babysitter sheet covers

SectionWhat to write
How to reach youBoth parents' phones, where you'll be, when you'll be home
The kidsAllergies, medicine, food, bedtime routine, what calms them down
Tonight's planDinner, bath, and bedtime with times
House rulesScreens, sweets, going outside, opening the door
In an emergency"Call 911 first," your home address, Poison Control, your pediatrician, a backup adult
Around the houseWi-Fi, first aid kit, flashlight, pajamas

US Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222. It's free and open 24 hours. Check the number for your country if you're outside the US.

Tips from parents

  • Be specific about allergies. "Peanuts, severe, EpiPen in the drawer by the fridge" is far more useful than "nut allergy."
  • Write down the bedtime "script." Kids test new sitters at bedtime. If the sheet says two books, the sitter can point to it.
  • Say what's normal. "Cries for 5 minutes after lights out, then sleeps" saves a nervous sitter from calling you mid-dinner.
  • Name a backup adult who can get there fast. A neighbor or relative who lives close is better than one who answers fast but lives an hour away.
  • Keep codes off the fridge. Give door and alarm codes in person or by text.

Questions

What's the most important thing to put on a babysitter sheet?
Your phone numbers, your home address, allergies, and any medicine. Those are the things a sitter needs fastest if something goes wrong. Fill in the emergency box first.
Why write my own address on it?
When people are stressed, they forget addresses, especially sitters who don't live nearby. A 911 dispatcher will ask for it first.
Where does my information go?
Nowhere. The sheet is made in your browser and nothing you type is uploaded. If "Save a draft on this device" is on, your answers stay in this browser so you can reuse them.
Can I use this for grandparents or a nanny?
Yes. Change the title to anything you like, such as "Notes for Grandma," and fill in what applies.
Does this give medical advice?
No. The boxes are for your own instructions, written the way your child's doctor told you. In an emergency, the sitter should call 911.
