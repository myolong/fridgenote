House rules for guests: friendly examples

Good house rules are short, specific, and say why when it isn't obvious. Below are examples for the rules hosts use most, in wording you can copy.

Updated 2026-11-20

Rules work best when guests see them as helpful information, not a warning. Compare "NO LOUD NOISE AFTER 10" with "Quiet hours are 10 PM to 8 AM. Our neighbors are close." Same rule, very different feeling.

Examples by topic

TopicExample wording
Quiet hoursQuiet hours are 10 PM to 8 AM. Our neighbors are close, so please keep music and voices low outside.
SmokingNo smoking or vaping inside or on the porch. Thanks for keeping the place fresh for the next guests.
PetsSorry, no pets. Some guests have allergies.
Pets (allowed)Dogs are welcome! Please keep them off the beds and pick up after them in the yard.
Number of guestsUp to 4 overnight guests. Please check with us before inviting visitors.
PartiesPlease, no parties or events.
ShoesPlease leave shoes by the door. Slippers are in the basket.
ParkingOne car in the driveway. Please don't block the neighbor's side.
KitchenHelp yourself to coffee, tea, and spices. Please run the dishwasher before you leave.
TowelsBeach towels are in the hall closet. Please don't take bath towels outside.
Hot tubPlease shower before using the hot tub and put the cover back on after.
FireplaceUse only the gas fireplace. Please don't burn wood or paper in it.
ThermostatPlease keep the thermostat between 66 and 76°F, and turn it down when you head out.
TrashTrash goes in the gray bin, recycling in the blue one. Both are beside the garage.
BreakagesAccidents happen. If something breaks, just let us know.
Locking upPlease lock the door whenever you leave, even for a short walk.
ChildrenKids are welcome. The stairs have no gate, so please keep an eye on little ones.
Check-in/outCheck-in is after 4 PM and checkout is by 10 AM so we have time to clean.
Outdoor lightsPlease switch off the patio lights at night. They attract bugs and bother the neighbors.
QuestionsIf anything's unclear, text us. We'd rather you ask than guess.

Tips for writing your own

  • Pick the rules that matter for your place. Skip the generic ones nobody would break.
  • Say "please" and give a reason when the reason isn't obvious.
  • Be specific. "Keep it down" is vague. "Quiet hours are 10 PM to 8 AM" is clear.
  • End on a friendly note. Telling guests to text with questions makes the rules feel less like a list of don'ts.

Questions

How many house rules should I have?
Five to eight covers most homes. A long list is harder to remember and can feel unwelcoming.
Should I explain the reason for a rule?
When it isn't obvious, yes. "Quiet hours are 10 PM to 8 AM. Our neighbors are close" gets more cooperation than the rule alone.
Where should I put house rules?
In the welcome book, and in a short version near the door if there's one rule that matters most, like no smoking.
