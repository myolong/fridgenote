Pet sitter checklist: what to leave for your sitter

The short version: food amounts, medicine, the daily routine, your vet and a 24-hour clinic, a backup contact nearby, and where the leash and carrier are. Here's the full list, with an example.

Updated 2026-11-20

Most problems with pet sitters come from missing information, not bad sitters. Someone who has never lived with your dog doesn't know that "a scoop" means the small red scoop, or that he always barks at the mail carrier and then settles down.

Use this checklist to make sure your sitter has what they need. You can copy it by hand, or fill in our free pet sitter sheet and print it.

The checklist

1. How to reach you

  • Your phone number, and whether a text or a call is better
  • Where you'll be staying
  • The dates you're away, including what time you'll be back

2. Each pet

  • Name, kind, age. Helpful if the sitter ever has to describe your pet to a vet.
  • Food and amount. Name the container and the scoop. "1 cup of dry food from the bin in the pantry" leaves no room for guessing.
  • Feeding times. Pets are happier when meals happen when they expect them.
  • Treats. How many are allowed. Some sitters show love with snacks.
  • Medicine. Copy it from the label: what, how much, when, and how you usually give it.
  • Habits and quirks. Hiding, barking, pulling on the leash, refusing food on the first day.
  • Things to never do. Foods to avoid, rooms that are off-limits, doors that must stay closed.

3. The daily routine

Write it with times. A sitter can follow "7 AM: breakfast and pill, short walk" much more easily than a paragraph. Our sheet prints this part with checkboxes so they can tick things off.

4. Walks, litter, and play

  • How long the walks are and where the leash and bags live
  • How often to scoop the litter box, and where the bags go
  • Favorite toys or games
  • Where your pet sleeps

5. Vet and emergencies

  • Your regular vet's name, phone, and address
  • The nearest 24-hour emergency clinic. Ask your vet which one they recommend.
  • A pet poison line your vet recommends
  • A backup contact who lives nearby and can get to your home
  • Where the carrier and any medical records are

Call your vet before you leave and let them know who will be caring for your pet. Some clinics will note it on your file.

6. Around the house

  • Where supplies are: food, towels, cleaning spray
  • Wi-Fi details (or a Wi-Fi QR card)
  • Trash day
  • House rules, like "no pets on the couch"

Before you leave

  • Stock up on food and medicine, with a few days extra in case you're delayed.
  • Walk through the routine with your sitter, ideally at feeding time.
  • Show them where the carrier, leash, and first aid supplies are.
  • Send door codes privately, not on the printed sheet.

Questions

How much detail is too much?
If your sitter would have to text you to ask, it belongs on the sheet. If it's something any adult would know, like where the sink is, leave it off. One page is a good target.
Should I leave instructions on paper or by text?
Both. A printed sheet on the fridge is easy to find and doesn't get buried. A PDF copy on their phone helps when they're out on a walk.
What if my pet has a medical condition?
Write the instructions exactly as your vet gave them, including doses and times, and add your vet's number. Ask your vet what signs should prompt a call.
