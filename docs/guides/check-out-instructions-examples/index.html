Check-out instructions: examples you can copy

Guests follow check-out steps when the list is short and each step is easy. Here is a 6-step example, plus wording for the most common requests.

Updated 2026-11-20

Most guests want to leave the place in good shape. What they don't know is what "good shape" means to you. Does the dishwasher need to run? Do towels go in the tub or stay on the rack? A short list answers those questions before anyone has to ask.

A 6-step example

This is the kind of list that fits on the back page of a welcome book or a small card by the door:

Check-out is 11 AM. Before you go:

  1. Load and start the dishwasher (pods are under the sink).
  2. Put used towels in the bathtub.
  3. Take kitchen trash to the gray bin by the garage.
  4. Set the thermostat to 65°F.
  5. Close and lock all windows and the back door.
  6. Leave the key on the kitchen counter and lock the front door behind you.

That's it. Thanks for staying with us, and safe travels!

Six steps, all doable in about ten minutes. Each one names a place ("under the sink", "by the garage") so nobody has to search.

Wording for common requests

TaskExample wording
TimeCheck-out is 11 AM. If you need a little longer, text us the day before and we'll do our best.
DishesPlease load and start the dishwasher. No need to unload it.
TowelsLeave used towels in the bathtub.
BedsNo need to strip the beds. We'll take care of it.
TrashKitchen trash goes in the gray bin by the garage. Recycling goes in the blue bin.
FridgePlease take your food with you, or toss anything opened.
Heat and ACSet the thermostat to 65°F in winter or 78°F in summer.
Lights and windowsTurn off the lights and close the windows, especially the one in the upstairs bathroom.
KeysLeave the key on the kitchen counter. The door locks automatically when you pull it shut.
Keypad doorJust close the door and press the lock button. Your code stops working at 11 AM.
Something brokeAccidents happen! Please let us know if anything broke so we can replace it before the next guests.

What to leave out

  • Full cleaning. Vacuuming, mopping, or cleaning the bathroom is usually the cleaner's job. Asking guests to do it can feel unfair if there's also a cleaning fee.
  • Long paragraphs. A numbered list is easier to follow while carrying bags.
  • Warnings in capital letters. "Please" and a short reason get better results than "DO NOT".

Where guests will see it

Put the list on the last page of your welcome book, under its own heading. Then print the same list as a small card and leave it by the front door or on the kitchen counter. Guests look for it on the morning they leave, not when they arrive.

Questions

How many check-out steps is too many?
More than seven starts to feel like a chore list. If something takes your cleaner five minutes, it's usually better to leave it to them.
Should I ask guests to strip the beds?
Some hosts do, some don't. If you ask, say where the used sheets go, for example "Please put used sheets in the basket by the washer."
Where should check-out instructions go?
On the last page of your welcome book, and as a short card by the front door, since that's where guests are standing when they leave.
